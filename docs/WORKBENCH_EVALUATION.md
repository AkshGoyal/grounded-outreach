## What testing found, and what was fixed

Before the fixes: 14 of 15 reached a draft, 10 had a Strong hook, 13 passed the checks on the first draft. Each problem was fixed, covered by a test, and the affected prospects were run again.

1. **Typed title 'Chief People Officer' did not match 'Executive Vice President, Chief People Officer' on the page, so the run stopped to ask which person.** Titles now match across rank, abbreviations and fuller forms; different jobs still differ. *(Found on Christina Schelling, Verizon.)*
2. **Verizon's own bio page puts the name and title on separate lines, so it never counted as proof of identity.** Profile pages with the name on one line and the title on the next now confirm identity. *(Found on Christina Schelling, Verizon.)*
3. **The appointment on yum.com was not treated as Yum! Brands' own site because the address is shorter than the company name.** A web address that is the start or the initials of the company name counts as the company's site. *(Found on Nai De Leon, Yum! Brands.)*
4. **The company's own 'pleased to announce' release was set aside as undated.** Announcements on the company's site or a wire service are dated by the day they were published; news sites still are not. *(Found on Christina Schelling, Verizon.)*
5. **Natural phrases ('That's why I'm writing', 'HR', 'probably', 'just as much') were blocked as unsupported claims, costing a second writing call.** The sender explaining their own email, the goal's own words and ordinary caution words no longer block. *(Found on Christina Schelling, Verizon.)*
6. **'Prior to joining Precisely…' was chosen as the hook over what he does now.** What the person does now ranks above where they worked before. *(Found on Joel Chaplin, Precisely.)*
7. **'Leading global IT' was blocked as a boast.** 'Leading a team' is allowed; 'a leading provider' is still blocked. *(Found on Joel Chaplin, Precisely.)*
8. **A departing executive's news, filed under the same label, made the appointment look like a date conflict.** News about different named people is never treated as one event. *(Found on Eric Stober, Innventure (before the evaluation).)*
9. **'A new finance chief often starts by…' was blocked as an uncautious claim.** 'Often', 'usually' and 'typically' count as caution for a general observation. *(Found on John Vandemore, Levi Strauss & Co. (before the evaluation).)*

## How this was measured

- Each prospect was run once as a rep would: name, company and title, Thorough research, with an email, using that goal. Runner: `scripts/workbench_evaluate.py`; prospects and goals: `evaluation/workbench_cases.json`.
- Each final email was read against the hook passage and its public source. Verdicts: send = would send as written; edit = one small wording change first; check = confirm a fact before sending. 'Wrong facts' counts any statement about the prospect or company that the source does not support. Reviewed by the builder (Claude, on Aksh's behalf), not by an independent SDR.
- "About 20 minutes by hand" is a typical manual research time per prospect; review time is not counted on either side.
- Costs are what the providers charged: $0.31 for the 15 final runs, $0.44 for all 21 runs including re-runs.
- Fifteen prospects is a small sample. It shows the process works across roles, companies and pitches; it does not measure reply rates.
